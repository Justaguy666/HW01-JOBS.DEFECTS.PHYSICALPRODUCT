## 200-300 Word Critique

AI was useful in this assignment mainly as an organizing and drafting assistant. It helped create repeatable folder structures, normalize Markdown templates, summarize QA/QC job postings, produce first-pass software-defect analyses, build Requirement 3 test-case documentation, export workbook summaries, scan references, and assemble the main report. This saved time on mechanical formatting and made the deliverables more consistent.

However, the work also showed clear limits. AI was less reliable when source evidence was incomplete or when a requirement depended on real-world proof. For Requirement 2, several defect sources were research papers or datasets rather than simple incident reports, so AI could easily overstate certainty. I had to keep uncertainty notes and avoid claiming exact root causes without evidence. For Requirement 3, AI could suggest plausible physical-product tests, but it could not replace actual execution evidence, photos, screenshots, GitHub issues, or video narration. The missing video evidence remains a disclosed exception.

My conclusion is that AI should be used for structure, drafting, comparison, and consistency checks, but not as the final authority. Human review is necessary for requirement interpretation, evidence validation, defect severity, acceptance decisions, privacy compliance, and final submission responsibility. In this homework, all 36 prompt-log entries were audited as individual prompt groups using the verdict recorded in the AI-02 audit appendix: 35 were VALID, 0 were INCOMPLETE, and 1 was INVALID.
